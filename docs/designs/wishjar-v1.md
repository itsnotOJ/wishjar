## Problem Statement
Birthday wishes arrive scattered across chat apps, social media and email, and get lost. Wishjar gives the celebrant one permanent jar, collected through one link, that opens on their birthday and fills again every year.

## Demand Evidence
None observed yet. The founder has not seen anyone in their circle save, screenshot, reread or mourn lost birthday wishes (Q1: "No behaviour seen yet"). This is the biggest open risk, and v1 is framed as an experiment to produce this evidence.

## Status Quo
Unknown. The founder hasn't observed what friends and family do with wishes after the day (Q2: "Not sure what happens"). The assumed status quo is quick messages in WhatsApp, Instagram and similar apps that cost the wisher two seconds and are never reread.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- No first celebrant has been named yet (Q3: "Nobody specific yet").
- First users: the founder's own friends and family, 3 to 5 people with birthdays coming up soon.
- The wedge is the midnight reveal: wishes collected in advance, hidden until the birthday, then opened at once. The permanent yearly archive is the reason to come back, not the reason to start.

## Constraints
- Solo founder. Stack is fixed: Next.js (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind CSS, Supabase (Postgres), Resend, Vercel.
- Accounts: Vercel exists but is not connected to the repo. Supabase, Resend and a sending domain are not set up.
- Vercel cron: the Hobby plan only allows jobs that run once per day, with ±59 minute timing. Pro and Enterprise allow per-minute schedules ([Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing)). Decision 10 needs an hourly job, so on Hobby the hourly email job runs from Supabase (`pg_cron` calling a protected API route) instead. See Dependencies.
- No accounts or passwords anywhere. Mobile first. Everything in "Out of scope for version one" in the brief stays out.

## Premises (agreed, D6)
1. Demand is unproven, so v1 is an experiment run on 3 to 5 real upcoming birthdays, judged on behaviour: did the celebrant share, did wishers write more than one line, did the celebrant come back.
2. The lid opening at midnight is the hook that gets the link shared. The archive is the reason to return. Share text, link preview and landing copy lead with the reveal.
3. The celebrant owns the jar. A friend can start a jar for someone and hands over a one-time claim link, never the private link (D2).
4. A wisher finishes in under a minute on one phone screen, with no account and tap-to-start prompts. Real messages matter more than the number of wishes.
5. Year two depends on a re-share nudge about two weeks before the birthday, plus a calendar entry. Email stays optional but is encouraged for that reason. (CEO D14: the nudge and calendar are deferred out of v1 to TODOS.md. They must ship 14 days before the earliest jar's second birthday.)
6. The product spreads through links in chat apps. The link preview is the shop window and must look good in WhatsApp and iMessage. Its copy is timeless ("[Name]'s birthday jar · Drop a wish in before it opens", plus the jar image), because chat apps cache previews and the link is reused every year. The live countdown appears only on the page (D5). Pages stay out of search engines.
7. (Amended by spec-review D4 and CEO D13, D14.) Phases 1 and 2 (jar, gift, email confirm, recovery) must be live before the first test jar is created. Phase 3 (birthday email) must be live before the first test birthday. That birthday sets the build deadline. Phase 4 is deferred out of v1.

## Decisions Added in This Session
- **D2. Who shares: celebrant owns, a friend can start.** Anyone can start a jar for someone else ("Start one for a friend"). Why: celebrants asking for their own wishes can feel like fishing for attention, and an empty jar is the most likely way v1 dies. **Gift access (spec review D3):** the friend never holds the private link. They get the public link to share, plus a one-time claim link to forward to the celebrant. When the celebrant opens the claim link, it mints their private link and the claim link stops working. Until it's claimed, the jar collects wishes normally, and nobody can read or manage it.
- **D3. Year two: early nudge plus calendar.** About 14 days before each birthday, send one email to a confirmed address prompting a re-share. "Add to my calendar" is available from the private view at any time, not only at creation (spec review D2, fix 6). The calendar file contains the public link only. Why: the brief's only email arrives on the birthday, too late to collect wishes. **This changes the brief's rule "email is used only for link backup and the birthday email"**: email is now used for link backup, the birthday email and the pre-birthday nudge.
- **D4. Wish form: tap-to-start prompts.** Three chips above the message box ("A memory of us", "What I love about you", "My wish for you") start the sentence when tapped. One screen: name, message, send. No minimum length. The draft is kept if the chat app reloads the page. Why: a blank box produces "hbd".
- **D8. Approach: full v1, phased by when each part is first needed** (see Recommended Approach).
- **D9. Wireframe: approved as "good enough"** (wireframe below). Layout details go to design review.

## Decisions From Spec Review (founder-approved)
- **Recovery (fix 1):** "I lost my link" emails a one-time claim link per jar, valid for 1 hour. The new private link is minted, and the old one cancelled, only when that claim link is used. Requesting recovery alone cancels nothing.
- **Birthday occurrences (fix 2):** each sealed wish belongs to a specific birthday occurrence of its jar, not a bare calendar year. If the celebrant changes the birthday or timezone, every still-sealed occurrence is re-resolved to the new date. Example: on 10 Nov 2026 the birthday moves from 3 Jan to 20 Dec, so wishes waiting for 3 Jan 2027 now open on 20 Dec 2026. Unlocked occurrences never change (decision 3). The 7-day late window follows the re-resolved date.
- **Email timing (fix 3):** a birthday email or nudge goes out on the first hourly run at or after 08:00 local time on its target day. That covers half-hour and 45-minute offsets and daylight-saving shifts.
- **Exactly once (fix 4):** each email is recorded per jar, per occurrence and per kind (birthday, nudge). The job claims a row before sending, so retries and overlapping runs never send twice. A missed run catches up later the same local day. After that day the email is skipped, not sent late.
- **Email in Phase 1 (fix 5):** the email field and its confirmation ship together in Phase 2. Jars created in Phase 1 add an email later from settings.
- **29 February (fix 7):** the calendar file uses a "last day of February" yearly rule, so it lands on 28 Feb in non-leap years. The birthday email and nudge follow the 28 Feb unlock.
- **Confirmation wording (D6):** every wish that will open later gets "Saved for [Name]'s next birthday", exactly as decision 1 says.
- **Wall toggle (D7):** switching from Private back to Wall re-shows the Wall-mode wishes from the most recent unlocked year. Wishes written under Private never appear on the Wall.
- **Measurement (D8):** a minimal event log in the app's own database records public page views per jar, private-view opens, invite taps ("Start my jar", "Start one for a friend") and time from form open to submit. No third-party trackers, no IP addresses or names stored. The privacy notice mentions it. Each success criterion maps to a query.

## CEO Review Decisions (founder-approved, /plan-ceo-review, HOLD SCOPE)
- **Riskiest assumption:** wishers will write *before* the birthday. Every reminder app fires on the day, so the jar could open nearly empty.
- **D17, early-wish copy:** the public page and share text name the deadline ("Opens at midnight on [date], get yours in before then"). Birthday-week copy must not make late wishers feel too late. The event log separates wishes sent before unlock from birthday-week wishes, so each test birthday shows the split.
- **D10, lost gift claim link (R2-2):** the friend who starts a gift jar also gets a gift-management link. It can only issue a fresh claim link, which cancels the previous one. It grants no read, edit or delete access, and stops working once the jar is claimed. An already-used claim link shows "This jar has been claimed".
- **D11, birthday moved to a passed date (R2-4):** if today falls within 7 days after the new date, still-sealed wishes unlock now as birthday week. Otherwise they move to the next occurrence of the new date. An occurrence that unlocks because of an edit gets no birthday email. Example: on 10 Nov the birthday changes from 3 Jan to 5 Nov, so waiting wishes open immediately (10 Nov is day 5 of birthday week).
- **D12, measuring shares (R2-6):** log taps on the private view's Share button. Links shared from there carry a short source tag (`?s=c` for links the celebrant shares, `?s=g` for links shared from the gift flow). Log page-load-to-submit time. Criterion-to-event map:
  - celebrant shared → Share-button tap from the private view, plus first page view with `s=c`
  - wishes longer than one sentence → computed from wish text at analysis time (no extra event)
  - link to confirmation under 60 s → page-load-to-submit time
  - invite taps → "Start my jar" and "Start one for a friend" tap events
  - celebrant opens within 24 h → first private-view open after unlock
- **D13, Phase 2 timing (R2-20):** Phase 2 must be live before the first test jar is created, so every test jar can have email backup and gift jars are available.
- **D14, Phase 4 deferred (R2-5):** the re-share nudge and calendar file move to TODOS.md. No nudge emails exist in v1, which closes R2-5.
- **D15, Wall kept:** the Wall stays in v1, as in the brief.
- **D16, public wish count dropped:** the public page never shows how many wishes a jar holds (removes Assumption 1).
- **D18, safety fixes:**
  - Recovery and confirmation emails are limited to 3 per email per hour and 10 per IP per hour. Confirmation links expire after 24 hours.
  - The wish rate limit is 5 per IP per jar per hour. A limited wisher keeps their draft and sees "Too many wishes from here right now, try again in a bit".
  - The scheduled-job route requires a shared secret stored in Supabase Vault and a Vercel environment variable. Only the `pg_cron` path is built unless the Vercel plan is Pro.
  - Rate-limit IPs are stored only as hashed keys with a 1-hour expiry, outside the event log, and the privacy notice says so.

## Approaches Considered
- **A. Minimal jar, no email:** rejected because it drops link recovery and the birthday email, so a lost private link loses a real jar.
- **B. Full v1, phased by first need:** chosen.
- **C. Hand-run pilot before any code:** rejected as the main path because it doesn't test the jar or reveal and waits on birthdays. Kept as a side activity during phase 1 (see The Assignment).

## Recommended Approach
Build everything in the brief, its 14 decisions and D2 to D4, in the order each part is first needed. Each phase can be deployed on its own.

```
PHASE 1  Core jar          create jar, public wish form (prompts, honeypot, limits),
                           sealing + 00:00 unlock (computed at read time, no job needed),
                           private view by year, Wall, settings, delete, link preview,
                           noindex, countdown and birthday-week states, event log (D8)
PHASE 2  Safety net        email field + confirm, gift a jar with claim link and
                           gift-management link (D10), "I lost my link" recovery
                           (identical response, old link cancelled only on claim),
                           email rate limits (D18)
PHASE 3  Birthday morning  hourly job: "Your jar is open" email, first run >= 08:00 local
-------  (deferred, D14)   re-share nudge ~14 days before; calendar file -> TODOS.md
```

- Deadline (CEO D13): Phases 1 and 2 are live before the first test jar is created. Phase 3 is live before the first test birthday.
- Data model: a `jar_occurrence` row holds one birthday occurrence of a jar, with its resolved unlock instant and whether it has unlocked (fix 2). A `wish` row holds text, name or null, `occurrence_id`, and the visibility mode it was written under (decision 5). The year is only a display label (Assumption 9). A separate `wish_attachment` table (empty in v1) makes photos and voice notes additive later.
- Wireframe: `docs/designs/wishjar-v1-wireframe.png` (wish form, confirmation and invite, celebrant view with lid on).

## Success Criteria
Measured on the first 3 to 5 test jars through the event log (D8). Thresholds are proposals, see assumptions:
- At least 3 celebrants share their link with no prompting from the founder beyond creating the jar.
- At least half of wishes are longer than one sentence.
- A wisher can go from opening the link to the confirmation in under 60 seconds on a phone.
- At least 1 wisher taps "Start my jar" or "Start one for a friend".
- On the birthday, every test celebrant opens their jar within 24 hours of unlock.
- Year two (measured next year): the celebrant re-shares within the nudge window.

## Reviewer Concerns

### R2-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> D2's new gift access rule says the friend never holds the private link and forwards a one-time claim link instead, but premise 3 (unchanged) still says 'A friend can start a jar for someone and hand over the private link.' The premises and the decision now describe two different gift mechanisms.

**Remedy**

> Amend premise 3 to say the friend hands over a one-time claim link, not the private link, matching D2.

### R2-2 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The gift claim link is a never-expiring bearer link passed through chat, and an unclaimed gift jar has no email (Assumption 3: the friend can't add one), so there is no recovery path. If the claim link is lost before the celebrant opens it, the jar keeps collecting wishes that nobody can ever read or manage, which is the outcome Approach A was rejected for ('a lost private link loses a real jar'). The design also doesn't say what happens if someone else (including the friend) opens the claim link first, or what the celebrant sees when they open an already-used claim link.

**Remedy**

> Define recovery for an unclaimed or wrongly claimed gift jar (for example, let the friend reissue a claim link from a gift-management link that grants no read access, cancelling the previous claim link, or let the friend enter the celebrant's email so the claim link goes there), and state what an already-used claim link shows.

### R2-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Fix 2 says each sealed wish belongs to a specific birthday occurrence, not a bare calendar year, but the Recommended Approach's data model still says a `wish` row holds 'the birthday year it belongs to'. Engineering planning would build the model that fix 2 rejects.

**Remedy**

> Change the data model to reference an occurrence (for example a `jar_occurrence` row with its resolved unlock instant and unlocked state, and `wish.occurrence_id`), with the year kept only as a display label (Assumption 9).

### R2-4 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> The re-resolution rule is defined only for moving the birthday to a date that is still ahead. If the new date has already passed this year, 're-resolved to the new date' is ambiguous. Example: on 10 Nov 2026 the birthday moves from 3 Jan to 5 Nov. The sealed occurrence could unlock immediately (5 Nov 2026 has passed, and 10 Nov falls inside the 7-day late window, so it is 'birthday week') or wait until 5 Nov 2027, about 12 months. The design also doesn't say whether the birthday email fires for an occurrence that unlocks on change.

**Remedy**

> State the rule for a new date that has already passed (for example: re-resolve to the next future occurrence of the new date, or unlock now if today falls inside the new date's 7-day window), with this worked example, and say whether the birthday email is sent or skipped.

### R2-5 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Assumption 5 allows a nudge in a jar's first year when the jar was created 14 or more days before the birthday. For a test jar created 3 weeks ahead, the first nudge fires 14 days before the first test birthday, so Phase 4's deadline comes before the Phases 1 to 3 deadline. That conflicts with the phase order ('phased by when each part is first needed') and with premise 7, the Assignment and Open Questions, which treat the first test birthday as the governing deadline.

**Remedy**

> Choose one: suppress first-year nudges for jars created before Phase 4 ships, or state that Phase 4's deadline is the first test birthday minus 14 days and reorder or merge the phases.

### R2-6 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The claim that each success criterion maps to a query is unsupported. The event log records public page views per jar, but views can't show who shared the link. For gift jars the friend shares by design, and the founder may share too, so 'at least 3 celebrants share their link with no prompting from the founder' can't be measured. 'Opening the link to the confirmation in under 60 seconds' is measured from form open, not link open, and 're-shares within the nudge window' has no event that separates a re-share from leftover traffic. This is R1-8's unmet obligation to say how each criterion is measured.

**Remedy**

> List each success criterion with its event and query, and add what's missing: for example, a share-button tap event on the private view, a `ref` or source parameter on links shared from the private view, and page-load-to-submit timing. Where a criterion stays founder-observed, say so.
